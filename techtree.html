DOCTRINE

A generated tech tree for Warzone 2100. Combat unlocks a topic; power researches it. Neither alone advances you.

The ratchet

Every weapon family has four axes and four tiers. Fighting reveals one random eligible cell at a time. A tier only becomes eligible once every axis of the tier below it has been researched — not merely unlocked. The dashed bars are those gates: you cannot cross one until all four cells to its left are paid for.

Weapon lineage

The guns themselves, and what each one costs you. Vanilla already encodes this succession and it is the best part of the existing tree — it just gated it on lab time. Under DOCTRINE the same prerequisites mean you must have fought with this family, because that is what unlocks a tier. You don't get the Heavy Machinegun from a lab; you get it because your MG crews reported what they needed.

How the families feed each other

Weapon lines are not islands — the vanilla tree already crosses them, and those crossings are the most interesting edges in the whole graph. Machineguns open the cannon line. Cannons plus flamers make the Plasma Cannon. Rockets become anti-air. Each arrow is a real prerequisite in the generated data.

What a kill is worth

Knowledge is weighted by what you actually killed, relative to your own army. Kill something far beneath you and you learn nothing — that is the anti-farming rule. Kill something above your weight and you learn disproportionately. Because the reference is your own 75th-percentile unit cost, it self-scales: the Viper that was a fair fight at minute five is worth zero by minute forty, with nothing to retune.

Value metric is droid.cost — calcDroidPower, the summed component build cost. A Dragon/Gauss/Tracks costs 602; a scavenger trike costs 38. That ratio is the tech gap, so no separate "tech level" needs inventing.

The fix, in one picture

The engine applies upgrades additively on the base value, so a constant value means a shrinking relative gain — vanilla's ninth cannon step delivers a third of the first step's effect while costing twelve times as much. DOCTRINE solves for values that hold the relative gain constant, so every tier feels like the one before it.

Vanilla runs nine tiers to reach +225% damage. DOCTRINE runs four to reach +144%, then spends the rest of the progression on range, rate of fire and accuracy — axes vanilla either barely touches or never touches at all.

The spine — the technologies that lead to this

The critical path. Everything depends on it, so it is early, cheap and the obvious first thing you do. Vanilla gives the Command Turret two topics out of 390; here it gets eight and becomes the backbone.

Beyond the ratchet

Signature topics apply to one specific weapon and stay invisible until you master it. Penetration raises the guaranteed-damage floor for one family, so rock-paper-scissors gets sharper as the game goes on. Infrastructure is gated on breadth — competence across several families, not depth in one.
